How this works

Every listing in Coinbase's public x402 Bazaar catalog is checked on a rotating schedule, about twice a day for most services. Each check asks for the resource without paying and records whether the server answered, how fast, and whether the payment terms it quotes match the listing: the same network, a wallet the listing (or the same service) names, and a price within 2× of what's listed. No wallet is involved and nothing is ever paid.

Scores weigh uptime (50%), speed (15%), whether the payment terms match the listing (20%), and paying customers in the last 30 days (15%, from the catalog). Grades need at least 3 checks in the last 7 days. Hosts with thousands of listings are sampled, so most of their listings stay ungraded. Paid-call counts and payer counts are Coinbase's own figures from the catalog.

What doesn't count against anyone: fresh payment addresses per request, paying into another wallet the same service lists, prices that vary by request (up to 2×), asking for input before quoting a price, route-pattern listings like /price/:address, rate limits, and TLS problems on our side.
