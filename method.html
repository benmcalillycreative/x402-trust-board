Method and limits

What the board checks, what its answers mean, and what they can't promise.

What is checked

Every endpoint listed in Coinbase's x402 Bazaar, Circle's Agent Marketplace and the MPP directory is asked for its price several times a day without paying. The answer (network, amount, the address to pay) is compared with the listing and with earlier answers. USDC payments to each service's payout addresses are counted on Base and Solana. Sellers can publish a signed payout proof.

Verdicts (check API, MCP tool and x402-trust-guard)

  • avoid: money would go somewhere it shouldn't. The endpoint switched to a wallet nothing it listed names, asks to be paid at a burn address or a non-public address, or asks for an address that contradicts the payout proof the seller published. Each is a direct observation of the endpoint's own 402 answer.
  • caution: it works, but something needs a look: a price well above its listing (often a stale listing), a wallet its listing doesn't name, flaky or slow answers, a C/D/F grade, too few checks to grade, or data more than a day old.
  • ok: graded A or B on recent data, with payment terms that match its listing and nothing contradicting its payout address.
  • unknown: the seller isn't in any catalog the board reads.

Grades (A–F) rank reliability and listing accuracy; they are not a safety verdict on their own.

What it can't tell you

  • Whether you'll get what you pay for. Checks never pay, so delivery after payment isn't tested.
  • Whether a seller is honest or solvent. A payout proof shows that whoever controls the domain also controls the wallet. Nothing more.
  • What happened since the last check. Data refreshes about every six hours; answers say how old they are, and anything over a day old is never "ok".
  • Payments elsewhere. Only USDC on Base and Solana is counted. Services that hand out a new address for every payment, or whose addresses aren't all counted yet, show no payment numbers rather than partial ones.

If the check can't be reached

x402-trust-guard lets the payer choose: onError: "allow" (default, the payment goes ahead) or "block". Unknown sellers and caution verdicts are allowed by default and can be blocked the same way.

How the numbers are counted

  • Headline counts cover endpoints listed now. Delisted ones stay in scores.json.
  • A service is a distinct host. Hosts paid at exactly the same addresses are shown as one service in payment tables, and every payment total counts each address once.
  • Paid-call counts come from Coinbase's catalog; payment counts come from the chain. They differ, and both are shown.

Independence and corrections

Grades and findings are never for sale: the seller plan changes how often and how closely a service is watched, never how it is graded. A seller who thinks a finding is wrong can reply to any board email; confirmed intended terms (an example offer, a price that depends on the request) are recorded as owner notes and shown as such. The code is public.
